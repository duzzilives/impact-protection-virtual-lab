Engineering for Impact Protection

Design, test, and refine a protective package for a sensitive electronic sensor. Investigate how mass, impact velocity, and stopping time affect average collision force.

NGSS HS-PS2-3Impulse & momentumEngineering design

Your engineering challenge

A technology company ships delicate medical sensors. Packages are sometimes dropped during transport, damaging the electronics. Your task is to design cushioning that reduces the average force on a sensor without using more than 12 cm of stopping distance.

Driving question: How can a protective device change a collision so that the sensor experiences less force?

Learning targets

  • Use Newton's second law and momentum ideas to explain collisions.
  • Investigate the impulse–momentum relationship.
  • Collect and interpret experimental data.
  • Evaluate and refine a design against criteria and constraints.

Physics model

|Favg| = m × |vf − vi| / Δt

For this test, final velocity is zero, so |Favg| = mv/Δt. With constant deceleration, stopping distance d = vΔt/2.

Model assumptions: one-dimensional impact, constant deceleration, sensor comes to rest, and force means average net force. Real force varies during impact; peak force can be higher.

5 · Engineering design decision

Choose a stopping time that reduces average force while meeting the 12 cm space limit. Then justify your proposed protective package with evidence from your recorded trials.

Design criteria: Reduce average force compared with the rigid-barrier control. Constraint: Stopping distance must be 12 cm or less.

6 · Check your understanding

1. What happens to average force when stopping time increases, if mass and velocity stay the same?

Average force decreases because the same momentum change occurs over a longer time interval.

2. Why does cushioning help protect the sensor?

Cushioning deforms during impact and can increase the time over which the sensor slows down. For the same change in momentum, a longer stopping time means a smaller average net force.

3. What happens if mass or impact velocity increases?

For the same stopping time, a larger mass or greater initial speed increases the magnitude of the momentum change and therefore increases average force.

4. Why is the smallest calculated force not always the best design?

The design must also meet constraints. In this lab, the stopping distance cannot exceed 12 cm. Real designs must also manage energy, material limits, cost, and protection of the object.

5. What is one limitation of this simulation?

It assumes constant deceleration and calculates average force. Real materials compress nonlinearly, forces change throughout the collision, and peak force may differ from the average.

Teacher notes · NGSS alignment

HS-PS2-3: Apply scientific and engineering ideas to design, evaluate, and refine a device that minimizes the force on an object during a collision.

  • Science: Newton's second law, momentum, impulse–momentum theorem.
  • Engineering practice: Define criteria and constraints; test, evaluate, and refine a solution.
  • Crosscutting concept: Cause and effect.
  • Suggested time: 55–75 minutes.
